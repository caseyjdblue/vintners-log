# Sanitation — Winemaking Reference Catalogue

**Catalogue:** Sanitation (Reference File 06)
**Scope of this edition:** How to clean and sanitize **everything** that touches the must or wine, which agents to use and at what strength, when to sanitize during the process, and the water prep that supports it — so spoilage and off-flavors never get a foothold.
**Status:** Living document — refine from your own setup and batch logs over time.

---

## How to use this file

This is the file that every **"sanitize"** instruction in the other catalogues points to. The Process & SOP catalogue tells you *when* to sanitize (it's built into the workflow); this file tells you *how*, *with what*, and *what exactly* to treat. The governing idea is simple and non-negotiable: **anything that touches the must or wine after it has cooled — every vessel, tool, and surface — must be sanitized first.** Most fruit-wine faults are preventable spoilage, and prevention is overwhelmingly cheaper than a rescue.

### Glossary / conventions

- **Cleaning vs. sanitizing:** *Cleaning* removes visible soil and residue. *Sanitizing* reduces microbes on an already-clean surface to a safe level. They are two separate steps — **you cannot sanitize a dirty surface**, because soil shields microbes and uses up the sanitizer.
- **Sanitize, not sterilize:** Home winemaking aims to sanitize (knock microbes down to where your yeast and sulfite win), not to achieve lab sterility.
- **No-rinse:** At the correct dilution, some sanitizers (Star San, iodophor) need no rinse — rinsing afterward with unsanitized water just re-contaminates. Follow the product's no-rinse dilution.
- **Contact time:** The minimum time a surface must stay wetted with sanitizer to work. Too brief = not sanitized.
- **Food-grade:** Use only food-grade containers and tools; non-food plastics can leach and harbor microbes.
- **ppm:** Parts per million — used for sanitizer strength as well as sulfite.

### The one rule

**Clean → rinse → sanitize → (no rinse) → use wet or drained.** Do it in that order, every time, for every contact item.

## Cleaners (step 1 — remove soil)

Clean immediately after use, when residue is soft; dried-on lees and pulp are far harder to remove and become microbe habitat.

- **Oxygen-based cleaner / PBW** (sodium percarbonate; e.g. PBW, or unscented OxiClean Free): the workhorse for vessels, tubing, and bottles. Warm-water soak loosens lees and stains; rinse well. Excellent on organic soil and gentle on equipment.
- **Unscented dish soap:** fine for general washing of tools; rinse thoroughly.
- **Avoid:** scented or antibacterial soaps (leave residue and off-aromas) and abrasive scrubbers/scouring pads on plastic — scratches in buckets, carboys, and tubing become permanent microbe refuges.

> Cleaning is not sanitizing. After cleaning and rinsing, you still must sanitize.

## Sanitizers (step 2 — reduce microbes)

| Sanitizer | Typical use dilution | Contact time | Rinse? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Star San** (acid-anionic) | ~1 oz per 5 gal (≈1.5 tsp/gal) | ~1–2 min | **No-rinse** | The home standard. Foams — "don't fear the foam." Mix with **RO/distilled water** for long solution life (weeks–months); tap water clouds it and shortens life but it still works short-term. Skin-safe at use strength. |
| **Sulfite (K-meta) sanitizing solution** | strong soak/spray, ~2 oz K-meta per gal of water | ~1–2 min | No-rinse (drain) | Reusable for a while; strong against wild yeast and mold, weaker against some bacteria. **Fumes — ventilate.** A *sanitizing* solution, far stronger than any must dose (see "three sulfite uses" below). |
| **Iodophor** (iodine) | ~12.5 ppm (per label, ~1 oz/5 gal) | ~1–2 min | No-rinse at correct dilution | Effective and no-rinse, but can stain plastics. |
| **Heat** (boil / oven / dishwasher) | boiling 10 min; oven glass ~60 min at ~340°F; dishwasher heat cycle, **no detergent/rinse-aid** | per method | n/a | For heat-safe glass/metal and bottles. Avoid thermal shock that cracks glass. |
| **Bleach** (unscented) | not recommended for winemaking | — | heavy rinse required | Risks chlorophenol off-flavors and corrosion; if ever used, dilute heavily and rinse thoroughly with non-chlorinated, sanitized water. **Default to Star San instead.** |

**Recommended default:** Star San for nearly everything, with a sulfite solution or heat as alternatives for specific items. Whatever you choose, respect the **dilution and contact time** — those two numbers are what make it work.

## The three different sulfite uses (don't confuse them)

Sulfite (K-meta / campden) shows up at three very different strengths across the catalogues. Mixing them up causes either ineffective sanitation or over-sulfited wine:

1. **Dechlorination** of water — *smallest* dose, ~1 campden tablet per **~20 gallons** (see Water prep below).
2. **Must / aging / bottling protection** — ~50 ppm (≈1 tablet/gal), with ~25 ppm top-ups (see Additives → Sulfite).
3. **Sanitizing solution** — *strongest*, ~2 oz K-meta per **gallon** of water, used as a soak/spray (this file).

Same chemical, three jobs, three very different concentrations.

## Sanitize EVERYTHING — the master checklist

If it touches the must or wine after cooling, it gets sanitized. Grouped to mirror the Equipment catalogue.

**Fermentation vessels**
- Primary bucket **and its lid** (and any spigot — disassemble and sanitize internally)
- Secondary carboy / demijohn / jug, inside and rim
- Airlock and the drilled bung/stopper (or grommeted lid)

**Fruit-prep tools**
- Straining/brewing bag
- Masher/spoon/stirrer, and the fruit press (every internal surface)
- Any bowl, scale tray, or surface that contacts the prepared fruit

**Transfer & racking**
- Auto-siphon / racking cane and **all tubing** (run sanitizer through the full length)
- Bottling wand, funnel, and its strainer

**Measurement instruments**
- Hydrometer **and test jar** (they go into the wine)
- Wine thief / sample taker
- Thermometer probe
- pH probe — sanitize gently, then **rinse with distilled water and return to storage solution**; don't soak a pH probe in Star San

**Yeast handling**
- Rehydration vessel and any stirrer/spoon, and anything that touches a yeast starter

**Bottling & closures**
- Bottles (sanitize and drain just before filling)
- Crown caps (Star San soak is fine); **natural corks: do not long-soak** in sanitizer (they wick and can contaminate) — use them from a sealed bag, a brief sulfite-solution dip, or steam
- Corker jaws / capper parts that contact the closure or bottle mouth

**You and your space**
- **Hands** — wash, then sanitize (Star San at use strength is skin-safe), or wear sanitized gloves
- Work surface and anything within drip range

> Vulnerable windows: the cool, low-alcohol stages are the riskiest — the must before active fermentation, and **cider and low-ABV/sweet wines** throughout. Alcohol and sulfite give *some* protection later, but never lean on that in place of sanitation.

## Water prep (synced with Process & SOP)

Use clean, **non-chlorinated** water. Western Washington municipal supplies are frequently treated with **chloramine** (and/or chlorine), which can cause medicinal/"band-aid" off-flavors (chlorophenols) and stress the yeast.

- **Dechlorinate with campden:** ~1 crushed campden tablet (or ~1/16 tsp K-meta) treats **~20 gallons** for dechlorination — it neutralizes both chlorine *and* chloramine. Add to the water and let stand a few minutes before use.
- **This is a different, much smaller dose than the ~50 ppm must-prep sulfite** in the Additives catalogue — don't confuse or double-count the two (see "three sulfite uses" above).
- **Alternatives:** carbon-filtered, spring, or RO water. Carbon filtering handles chlorine but is unreliable on chloramine; RO/distilled is mineral-free. Boiling drives off chlorine but **not** chloramine reliably — campden is the dependable fix for chloramine.
- Cider uses no added water, so this step is N/A for cider.
- **Sanitation note:** use low-mineral (RO/distilled) water to mix **Star San** for the longest solution life; chlorinated water also undermines sanitation by leaving chlorophenol off-flavors.

> **Sync note:** the canonical version of this water-prep guidance lives in **Process & SOP → Water prep**; the dechlorination dose and method here are mirrored from it. If either is updated, update both so they stay identical.

## What sanitation prevents (the spoilage organisms)

Sanitation, paired with sulfite and low oxygen, is what keeps these out (cross-references the SOP troubleshooting table):

- **Wild yeast** — off-flavors, hazes, unpredictable ferments. Prevented by sanitation + a strong pitched-yeast start + sulfite (and EC-1118's killer factor on foraged fruit).
- **Acetobacter (vinegar / acetic):** needs oxygen — prevented by sanitation, keeping headspace low, sealing vessels, and excluding fruit flies. Largely uncurable once established.
- **Lactic bacteria (Lactobacillus/Pediococcus):** sourness, ropiness, "geranium" when combined with sorbate-without-sulfite. Prevented by sanitation + sulfite.
- **Mold:** on fruit and in scratched/poorly-cleaned gear. Prevented by clean ripe fruit, sanitation, and avoiding scratched plastic.

The throughline: **clean gear + sanitized contact surfaces + sulfite + low oxygen** closes every common door at once.

## Do's and don'ts

- **Do** clean immediately after use, while residue is soft.
- **Do** keep a tub of Star San made up during active work and re-dip tools as you go.
- **Do** sanitize the *receiving* vessel and the *full length* of tubing before every transfer.
- **Don't** rinse a no-rinse sanitizer off with unsanitized water — that re-contaminates.
- **Don't** scratch plastic; replace scratched buckets/tubing.
- **Don't** rely on alcohol or sulfite in the wine to make up for skipped sanitation, especially for cider and low-ABV/sweet styles.
- **Don't** soak natural corks or a pH probe in Star San.
