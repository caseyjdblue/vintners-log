# Yeast Strains — Winemaking Reference Catalogue

**Catalogue:** Yeast Strains (Reference File 02)
**Scope of this edition:** The nine strains referenced in the Fruit Profiles catalogue — Lalvin 71B, RC212, EC-1118, BM4x4, Pasteur Red, QA23, Côte des Blancs, plus Mangrove Jack's M02 and White Labs WLP775 (cider).
**Status:** Living document — add strains and refine notes from your own batch logs over time.

---

## How to use this file

Each strain follows the same template so profiles stay comparable and cross-reference cleanly with the Fruit Profiles and Additives catalogues. Specs given are typical manufacturer figures; real-world performance shifts with temperature, nutrient level, sugar concentration, and pitch health, so treat them as planning starting points and confirm against your own ferments.

### Glossary / conventions

- **Species:** Most wine strains are *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*; high-tolerance/champagne strains are often *S. cerevisiae* var. *bayanus*, which ferment cleaner at extremes and tolerate more alcohol.
- **Alcohol tolerance:** The approximate ABV a strain reliably reaches before stalling. Exceed it and the ferment sticks with residual sugar. Pick a strain whose tolerance comfortably exceeds your target ABV.
- **Temperature range / optimal:** Within range the strain ferments cleanly. Too warm → fusel alcohols, harsh/solventy notes, blown-off aroma. Too cool → sluggish or stuck (though some strains are *built* for cool, aroma-preserving ferments).
- **Attenuation / dryness:** How completely the strain consumes sugar. High attenuation → lower FG, drier wine. A few strains intentionally finish a touch early, leaving body or slight sweetness.
- **Nutrient demand:** Low / medium / high nitrogen requirement. High-demand strains (e.g. RC212) will throw H₂S (rotten-egg sulfur) or stick if under-fed — pair them with a proper nutrient regimen. Fruit musts are nutrient-poor, so err toward feeding.
- **Killer factor:** "Killer" strains (e.g. EC-1118) secrete toxins that suppress wild/competing yeast, helping them dominate the must. Useful for foraged fruit.
- **Flocculation / lees compaction:** How tightly the yeast settles after fermentation — affects how easily the wine clears and racks.

### Pitching & rehydration (applies to all dry strains here)

1. **Rehydrate** dry yeast in ~10× its weight of clean water at ~104°F / 40°C (warm, not hot). Sprinkle on top, wait 15–20 min, stir gently. Optionally rehydrate with a rehydration nutrient (e.g. GoFerm) for a stronger start.
2. **Temper** by adding a little must to the slurry to close the temperature gap before pitching (avoid >18°F / 10°C cold shock).
3. **Pitch rate:** roughly 1 g dry yeast per gallon. A standard 5 g sachet handles ~5 gal.
4. **Liquid yeast** (WLP775) is pitched directly per the vial; make a starter if pitching into a larger or higher-gravity batch.

## Lalvin 71B (71B-1122)

### Overview
The fruit-winemaker's workhorse. Known for producing rounded, fruit-forward, ester-rich wines meant to be enjoyed relatively young. Its signature trick: it metabolizes a meaningful share of malic acid, softening sharp or (in cherry's case) flabby-but-thin acid profiles.

- **Species:** *S. cerevisiae*
- **Alcohol tolerance:** ~14%
- **Temp range:** 59–86°F; practical optimal 64–77°F
- **Speed:** fast, low foaming
- **Dryness:** ferments dry
- **Nutrient demand:** low–medium
- **Flavor/aroma:** emphasizes fresh fruit and esters; rounds and softens
- **Best for (our catalogue):** sweet cherry (top pick), strawberry, blueberry, blackberry, soft ciders — anywhere you want fruit front and center

### Watch-outs
- Only ~14% tolerance — not for high-ABV dessert/port styles (use EC-1118 there).
- Don't overage 71B wines; their charm is youthful fruit, which fades with long aging.

## Lalvin RC212 (Bourgovin)

### Overview
A Burgundy red strain that builds color and tannin structure while still respecting fruit character — the go-to when you want a berry "country wine" to read like a real red. The trade-off is appetite: it is a **high-nutrient** strain and will reward or punish your nutrient regimen accordingly.

- **Species:** *S. cerevisiae*
- **Alcohol tolerance:** ~16%
- **Temp range:** 59–86°F; optimal warm 68–86°F
- **Speed:** moderate
- **Dryness:** ferments dry
- **Nutrient demand:** **high** — feed it or expect H₂S / stuck ferment
- **Flavor/aroma:** enhances color extraction, structure, and red-fruit complexity
- **Best for (our catalogue):** blueberry, blackberry, structured cherry reds

### Watch-outs
- Stage nutrient additions (e.g. at pitch and at ~1/3 sugar break) to avoid sulfur.
- Likes warmth — sluggish at the cold end of its range.

## Lalvin EC-1118 (Prise de Mousse)

### Overview
The bulletproof champagne strain. Vigorous, fast, killer-factor, tolerant of temperature and alcohol extremes, and the standard choice for restarting stuck ferments, making sparkling wine/cider, and pushing high-ABV dessert and port styles. The cost of that reliability is neutrality — it tends to ferment very dry and can strip delicate aroma.

- **Species:** *S. cerevisiae* var. *bayanus*
- **Alcohol tolerance:** ~18%+
- **Temp range:** very wide, 50–86°F
- **Speed:** very fast, low foaming, compact lees
- **Dryness:** bone dry
- **Nutrient demand:** low; tolerant of poor conditions
- **Flavor/aroma:** clean and neutral — preserves little, adds little
- **Best for (our catalogue):** high-ABV cherry/blackberry dessert & port styles, dry/sparkling cider, foraged fruit (killer factor dominates wild yeast), any stuck-ferment rescue

### Watch-outs
- Can outcompete subtle aromatics — not the choice when delicate fruit expression is the goal (strawberry).
- Its vigor means it ferments dry fast; for off-dry styles plan to stabilize and back-sweeten.

## Lalvin BM4x4

### Overview
Selected for red wines where mouthfeel, body, and color stability matter. Tends to add roundness and a fuller midpalate while keeping good aromatic complexity — a step toward "structured but generous."

- **Species:** *S. cerevisiae*
- **Alcohol tolerance:** ~16%
- **Temp range:** 64–86°F; optimal 68–82°F
- **Speed:** moderate
- **Dryness:** ferments dry
- **Nutrient demand:** medium–high
- **Flavor/aroma:** enhanced body/mouthfeel, color stability, aromatic depth
- **Best for (our catalogue):** blueberry (body + color), robust blackberry reds

### Watch-outs
- Like RC212, feed it adequately.
- Prefers warmth; not a cool-ferment strain.

## Red Star Pasteur Red (Premier Rouge)

### Overview
A dependable strain for full-bodied red wines that develops varietal/fruit character and good color over a steady, moderate ferment. A solid, less nutrient-hungry alternative to RC212 for the berry reds.

- **Species:** *S. cerevisiae*
- **Alcohol tolerance:** ~15%
- **Temp range:** 64–86°F; optimal 70–85°F
- **Speed:** moderate
- **Dryness:** ferments dry
- **Nutrient demand:** medium
- **Flavor/aroma:** full body, developed fruit and color in reds
- **Best for (our catalogue):** blackberry (excellent), blueberry, full-bodied cherry reds

### Watch-outs
- Wants warmth to perform; slows noticeably at the cool end.

## Lalvin QA23

### Overview
A cool-fermenting strain bred for crisp, aromatic white wines — it lifts citrus/tropical thiol aromatics and stays clean and fast at low temperatures, while producing little H₂S. In a fruit-wine context it's the tool for preserving the delicate, fade-prone aromatics of light fruit.

- **Species:** *S. cerevisiae* var. *bayanus*
- **Alcohol tolerance:** ~16%
- **Temp range:** 50–68°F is where it shines (can ferment warmer, but you lose its cool-aroma advantage)
- **Speed:** fast even when cool, low foaming
- **Dryness:** ferments dry
- **Nutrient demand:** low, low H₂S producer
- **Flavor/aroma:** aromatic lift, crisp, fresh
- **Best for (our catalogue):** strawberry (preserve aroma with a cool ferment), light/fruity cider

### Watch-outs
- Ferment it cool to get the benefit — pitching warm wastes its strength.

## Lalvin Côte des Blancs (Epernay 2)

### Overview
A slow, low-temperature strain prized for retaining fruitiness in light and off-dry wines. Because it ferments slowly and at a lowish tolerance, a cool ferment can finish with a touch of residual sweetness — useful (intentionally) for fruit-forward, slightly sweet styles.

- **Species:** *S. cerevisiae*
- **Alcohol tolerance:** ~14%
- **Temp range:** ferments well cool, 50–68°F
- **Speed:** slow (a feature, not a bug — slow + cool preserves aroma)
- **Dryness:** high attenuation if warm, but cool ferments can finish slightly sweet
- **Nutrient demand:** low
- **Flavor/aroma:** preserves delicate fruit, fruity finish
- **Best for (our catalogue):** strawberry, light/off-dry fruit wines

### Watch-outs
- Slow ferments need patience and clean conditions over the longer window.
- If you want guaranteed dry, ferment warmer or choose a higher-attenuation strain.

## Mangrove Jack's M02 (Cider)

### Overview
A purpose-built cider strain that produces a fresh, fruity, crisp cider with good fruit retention — a fruitier result than a champagne yeast, with reliable, moderate attenuation.

- **Species:** *S. cerevisiae*
- **Alcohol tolerance:** ~12%
- **Temp range:** 57–79°F; optimal 64–79°F
- **Speed:** moderate
- **Dryness:** medium–dry
- **Nutrient demand:** medium
- **Flavor/aroma:** fresh, fruity, crisp; preserves apple character
- **Best for (our catalogue):** hard cider (first choice for a fruit-forward, sessionable cider)

### Watch-outs
- Ferment cooler for cleaner, fruitier results; warm ferments push it harsher.

---

## White Labs WLP775 (English Cider)

### Overview
A liquid strain that makes classic medium-dry English-style cider. Often throws some sulfur during active fermentation that cleans up with time, and leaves a balanced, traditional profile.

- **Species:** *S. cerevisiae*
- **Alcohol tolerance:** ~12% (moderate)
- **Temp range:** 68–75°F
- **Speed:** moderate
- **Dryness:** medium–dry (leaves balance and body)
- **Nutrient demand:** medium
- **Flavor/aroma:** traditional, balanced English cider; some transient sulfur during ferment
- **Best for (our catalogue):** hard cider (traditional medium-dry style)

### Watch-outs
- Liquid yeast — pitch fresh, consider a starter for larger batches, and mind viability/date.
- Early sulfur is normal; give it time and good nutrient rather than panicking.

## General notes & cross-references

- **Match tolerance to target.** Choose a strain whose alcohol tolerance sits comfortably above your target ABV. Going dessert/port (15%+)? EC-1118. Staying at a soft 10–12%? 71B / cider strains are fine.
- **Feed the hungry strains.** RC212 and BM4x4 are the high-nutrient ones in this set; staged nutrient additions prevent sulfur and stuck ferments — fruit musts are already nitrogen-poor.
- **Temperature is a flavor lever.** Cool ferments (QA23, Côte des Blancs) preserve delicate aromatics; warm ferments (RC212, Pasteur Red, BM4x4) extract color/structure in reds. Pick the strain *and* the temperature together.
- **Rehydrate properly.** The single cheapest quality improvement — proper rehydration temperature and an optional rehydration nutrient give a clean, fast start and fewer faults.
- **One strain per batch.** Don't blend strains in a single ferment unless deliberately experimenting; killer strains (EC-1118) will suppress the others.
