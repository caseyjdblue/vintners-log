# Additives — Winemaking Reference Catalogue

**Catalogue:** Additives (Reference File 03)
**Scope of this edition:** Every additive referenced in the Fruit Profiles and Yeast Strains catalogues — sulfite (Campden / K-meta), pectic enzyme, rehydration nutrient, yeast nutrient & energizer, acid blend, acid reducer, wine tannin, fermentable sugar (chaptalization), potassium sorbate, and core finings.
**Status:** Living document — refine doses and timing from your own batch logs over time.

---

## How to use this file

This is the catalogue that ties the others together: the Fruit Profiles tell you *which* additives a fruit needs, the Yeast Strains tell you *which* strains are nutrient-hungry, and this file tells you *what each additive does, how much, and exactly when*. Each additive follows the same template for consistency. Doses are typical home-scale starting points (per US gallon of must/wine); always confirm against measurement and your own results.

### Glossary / conventions

- **ppm:** Parts per million (mg/L). The standard way sulfite is dosed.
- **SO₂ — free vs. molecular:** *Free* SO₂ is the reserve available to protect the wine; *molecular* SO₂ is the small active fraction that actually does the antimicrobial work. The split depends on **pH** — see the sulfite-by-pH table below. Target ~0.8 ppm molecular SO₂.
- **YAN:** Yeast Assimilable Nitrogen — the nitrogen the yeast can actually use. Fruit musts are low in it; that's why nutrient matters.
- **TA / pH:** Titratable acidity (as % tartaric) and pH, from the Fruit Profiles. Acid additions target TA; pH drives sulfite dosing.
- **SG / OG / FG:** Specific / original / final gravity (hydrometer). Sugar additions target OG.
- **"Stage" / "staged":** Splitting an addition across the process (notably nutrient) rather than dumping it all at once.

### Two rules that prevent most additive mistakes

1. **Sulfite, then wait.** Sulfite added at must prep must dissipate ~12–24 h before pitching, or it suppresses your chosen yeast.
2. **Sorbate never flies solo, and never during fermentation.** Potassium sorbate is only for a finished, cleared wine, and must always be co-added with sulfite (sorbate alone + stray bacteria → "geranium" taint).

## Master addition timeline

The integrating view — what goes in at each stage. Cross-references to the other catalogues in the right column.

| Stage | Additive(s) | Purpose | Ties to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Must prep (Day 0)** | Sulfite (~50 ppm); pectic enzyme; sugar to target OG; acid blend to target TA; tannin (if pre-ferment extraction) | sanitize, de-pectin, set sugar/acid/structure | Fruit pH & TA & OG targets |
| **Wait 12–24 h** | *nothing* — let SO₂ dissipate | so sulfite won't inhibit pitched yeast | — |
| **Pitch (Day 1)** | Rehydration nutrient (in the yeast's rehydration water); 1st staged nutrient dose | strong, clean yeast start | Yeast: rehydration section |
| **~1/3 sugar break (days in)** | 2nd staged nutrient dose | prevent H₂S / stuck ferment | Yeast: RC212, BM4x4 (high demand) |
| **Sluggish / stuck** | Yeast energizer; restart with EC-1118 | rescue | Yeast: EC-1118 |
| **Racking / bulk aging** | Sulfite top-up (~25 ppm, ~every 3 mo) | prevent oxidation & spoilage | aging windows in Fruit Profiles |
| **Clearing** | Finings if needed; pectic enzyme if pectin haze persists | clarity | Fruit: high-pectin fruits |
| **Stabilize (before sweetening)** | Potassium sorbate **+** sulfite | prevent refermentation | Fruit: back-sweetening notes |
| **Back-sweeten (optional)** | Fermentable sugar / juice to taste | style | Fruit: strawberry, cider, blackberry |
| **Bottling** | Sulfite (~50 ppm); final acid/tannin palate tweaks | protect & balance | all |

> **Cider exception:** skip must-prep sulfite if using pasteurized, preservative-free juice and pitching immediately; and if bottle-conditioning for sparkle, **do not** use sorbate (it blocks the refermentation that carbonates the bottle). See Fruit Profiles → Apple (Hard Cider).

## Sulfite by pH (why cherry needs more than blackberry)

To hold ~0.8 ppm *molecular* SO₂ (the protective target), the *free* SO₂ you must maintain rises sharply with pH. This is a direct tie-in to the pH values in the Fruit Profiles.

| Wine pH | Approx. free SO₂ to maintain | Our fruits near this pH |
|---|---|---|
| 3.0 | ~13 ppm | blackberry (low end) |
| 3.2 | ~22 ppm | blueberry, blackberry |
| 3.4 | ~35 ppm | blueberry, strawberry, cider |
| 3.6 | ~56 ppm | cider |
| 3.8 | ~90 ppm | **sweet cherry** |
| 4.0 | ~140 ppm | sweet cherry (high end) |

**Takeaway:** the high-pH, low-acid fruits (sweet cherry above all) are the hardest to protect and need both diligent sanitation *and* more sulfite — or an acid addition to pull pH down into a safer, more sulfite-efficient range. The low-pH berries protect easily with modest sulfite.

## Sulfite — Campden tablets / Potassium metabisulfite (K-meta)

### Overview
The backbone preservative: antimicrobial (suppresses wild yeast and bacteria) and antioxidant (prevents browning/oxidation). Used at three points — must prep, during aging/racking, and at bottling — plus as an equipment sanitizer at higher concentration.

- **Form:** Campden tablets (convenient, 1 tablet ≈ 1 gal) or potassium metabisulfite powder (cheaper, more precise by weight). Potassium meta is preferred over sodium meta for wine.
- **Typical dose:** ~50 ppm at must prep and bottling; ~25 ppm maintenance at racking. Adjust to pH (see table).
- **Timing:** must prep (then **wait 12–24 h** before pitching); every ~3 months / at racking during bulk aging; at bottling.
- **Ties to:** Fruit Profiles (every fruit's must prep; pH-driven dosing); cider exception above.
- **Watch-outs:** never add right before pitching live yeast; don't overshoot (sulfur smell, sensitivity in some people); higher pH needs more to be effective.

## Pectic Enzyme (pectinase)

### Overview
Breaks down pectin in the fruit. Three benefits: more juice/color/flavor released, and — critically — it prevents the permanent pectin haze that fruit wines are prone to. For these fruits it's close to mandatory.

- **Form:** powder (most common) or liquid.
- **Typical dose:** ~1/2 tsp powder per gallon.
- **Timing:** at must prep, pre-fermentation (it works best before alcohol is present; high alcohol inhibits it). Works faster warm. Can be added post-ferment to chase a pectin haze, but far less effective once wine is alcoholic.
- **Ties to:** Fruit Profiles — essential for strawberry, blueberry, blackberry; recommended for cherry; optional for cider (some leave pectin for body, others add for clarity).
- **Watch-outs:** add it *early*; chilled fruit slows the enzyme; don't expect post-ferment additions to fully clear an established haze.

## Rehydration Nutrient (GoFerm-type)

### Overview
Added to the yeast's **rehydration water** (not the must). It loads the cells with sterols and micronutrients so they build strong membranes before facing the must — better alcohol tolerance, faster clean start, fewer faults. It is consumed during rehydration and is *not* a must nitrogen source.

- **Form:** powder, dissolved in the warm rehydration water.
- **Typical dose:** ~1.25 g per 1 g of yeast.
- **Timing:** at pitch only, in the ~104°F rehydration water (cool to 104°F before adding yeast).
- **Ties to:** Yeast Strains → "Pitching & rehydration"; biggest payoff for high-ABV / stressful ferments (EC-1118 dessert styles).
- **Watch-outs:** don't confuse with must nutrient — this one does its job before the yeast ever hits the must.

## Yeast Nutrient (DAP & complex blends)

### Overview
Supplies the nitrogen and micronutrients the yeast need to ferment cleanly to completion. Fruit musts are nutrient-poor, so this is the single most common fix for stuck/sluggish ferments and for H₂S (rotten-egg) sulfur off-aromas.

- **Form:** DAP (diammonium phosphate — pure inorganic nitrogen) or complex/organic blends (amino acids + vitamins + sterols, e.g. Fermaid-type). Complex blends are gentler and more complete; many "yeast nutrient" products are a mix.
- **Typical dose:** ~1 tsp per gallon total, **staged**.
- **Timing:** split the dose — part at pitch, part at the ~1/3 sugar break (after the ferment is established). Avoid adding nitrogen late (past ~2/3 sugar consumed): leftover nitrogen feeds spoilage organisms.
- **Ties to:** Yeast Strains — **RC212 and BM4x4 are the high-demand strains**; staged nutrient is what keeps them from throwing sulfur or sticking. Pasteur Red / cider strains are medium demand; 71B is low–medium; EC-1118 / QA23 / Côte des Blancs are low.
- **Watch-outs:** don't dump it all at pitch; don't add DAP late; more isn't better past the yeast's needs.

## Yeast Energizer

### Overview
A more potent kick than nutrient — typically DAP plus magnesium, vitamins, and yeast hulls. Used to wake up sluggish ferments, restart stuck ones (alongside an EC-1118 restart culture), and to fortify especially tough/high-gravity musts up front.

- **Form:** powder blend.
- **Typical dose:** ~1/2 tsp per gallon.
- **Timing:** at the first sign of sluggishness, as part of a stuck-ferment rescue, or proactively at pitch for high-ABV dessert musts.
- **Ties to:** Yeast Strains → EC-1118 (stuck-ferment rescue); high-ABV cherry/blackberry port styles.
- **Watch-outs:** energizer is a fix, not a substitute for proper nutrient timing and temperature control.

## Acid Blend

### Overview
Raises titratable acidity to brighten and balance a flat must. A blend of tartaric, malic, and citric acids; tartaric is the most stable wine acid.

- **Form:** powder (blended), or single acids if you want control.
- **Typical dose:** ~1 tsp per gallon raises TA roughly ~0.1% (approximate — measure).
- **Timing:** adjust toward target TA pre-fermentation, then fine-tune to taste pre-bottling (final palate balance is best judged on finished wine).
- **Ties to:** Fruit Profiles — **sweet cherry is the headline case** (low acid, high pH → needs acid both for balance and to make sulfite more effective). Strawberry usually fine; blueberry/blackberry often need none — measure first.
- **Watch-outs:** citric can be metabolized by bacteria (less stable); favor tartaric for stability. Don't over-acidify — easier to add than remove.

---

## Acid Reducer (Potassium Bicarbonate / Calcium Carbonate)

### Overview
The counterpart to acid blend — lowers TA when a wine is too sharp. Useful for the high-acid berries in an aggressive year.

- **Form:** potassium bicarbonate (preferred, less chalky) or calcium carbonate.
- **Typical dose:** per kit, in small increments — overshooting flattens the wine.
- **Timing:** post-fermentation, tasting as you go; cold stabilization will also drop some tartaric naturally.
- **Ties to:** Fruit Profiles — blackberry (TA up to ~1.2%) is the most likely candidate if a batch tastes austere.
- **Watch-outs:** calcium carbonate can leave a chalky taste and needs time to settle; go slow and taste.

## Wine Tannin (grape tannin / berry tannin)

### Overview
Adds structure, mouthfeel, light astringency, and color stability, and helps clarification by aiding protein/sediment settling. The fix for fruits that lack their own tannin.

- **Form:** powdered grape tannin (general), or specialized fermentation/finishing tannins.
- **Typical dose:** ~1/4–1/2 tsp per gallon for low-tannin fruit.
- **Timing:** **pre-ferment** for color/structure and extraction; **at aging** for a finishing mouthfeel adjustment.
- **Ties to:** Fruit Profiles — strawberry (very low tannin) and sweet cherry benefit most; blueberry optionally; **skip for blackberry** (already tannic).
- **Watch-outs:** easy to overdo into harsh astringency; add in small steps and taste, especially as a finishing addition.

## Fermentable Sugar (Chaptalization)

### Overview
Raises the must's gravity to reach target ABV. Every fruit here except fresh-pressed cider needs it to hit table-wine strength.

- **Form:** cane/beet sucrose (clean, standard default); dextrose/corn sugar (ferments fully); honey (adds mead-like character); brown sugar/molasses (flavor). Cane is the default.
- **Typical dose/rule:** ~1 lb cane sugar in 1 gallon raises SG by ~0.045. Add gradually, confirm with hydrometer.
- **Timing:** pre-ferment to target OG. For **high-ABV dessert/port** styles, add in stages (step-feeding) to avoid osmotic stress and stuck ferments.
- **Ties to:** Fruit Profiles OG targets (~1.085–1.095 for table wines; 1.110+ dessert; ~1.050–1.060 cider) and Yeast Strains alcohol tolerances (don't set an OG your strain can't finish).
- **Watch-outs:** match target ABV to yeast tolerance, or the ferment sticks sweet. Don't dissolve all the sugar at once for very high-gravity musts.

## Potassium Sorbate (stabilizer)

### Overview
Prevents yeast from *reproducing*, stabilizing a finished wine against refermentation — the prerequisite for safely back-sweetening. It does **not** kill existing yeast, so the wine must already be fully fermented and racked clear.

- **Form:** powder.
- **Typical dose:** ~1/2 tsp per gallon.
- **Timing:** only on a finished, cleared wine, immediately before back-sweetening — and **always co-dosed with sulfite**.
- **Ties to:** Fruit Profiles back-sweetening notes (strawberry, blackberry, cider); **the cider conflict** — sorbate blocks bottle-conditioning, so choose sparkling-via-priming *or* stabilized-and-sweetened, never both.
- **Watch-outs:** sorbate without sulfite + stray lactic bacteria → "geranium" off-smell (ruins the batch); never add to an active ferment; ineffective if heavy yeast remains.

## Finings / Clarifiers

### Overview
Agents that drop haze and suspended solids for a bright, clear wine. Pectic enzyme and tannin already help; these are the dedicated clarifiers when a wine won't clear on its own (after giving it time and cold settling first).

- **Bentonite:** clay; removes proteins/haze. Can be used pre-ferment or for clearing. Effective but compacts a lot of lees (wine loss).
- **Sparkolloid:** general-purpose, easy, good for stubborn hazes; bulky lees.
- **Kieselsol + Chitosan:** popular two-part system (negative then positive charge) — fast, tight lees, low wine loss.
- **Isinglass / Gelatin:** fine polish; gelatin also reduces excess tannin/astringency.
- **Typical dose/timing:** per package, late in aging once the wine is otherwise done; rack off the lees afterward.
- **Ties to:** Fruit Profiles high-pectin fruits (use pectic enzyme *first*, finings only if haze persists); gelatin pairs with over-tannic blackberry.
- **Watch-outs:** time + cold often clears a wine without finings; over-fining strips flavor and color; always rack promptly off fining lees.

## Per-fruit additive package (cheat sheet)

The direct tie-in: the recommended additive kit for each fruit in this edition. "Optional" = style-dependent.

| Fruit | Must prep | During ferment | Finishing / stabilizing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sweet cherry** | sulfite (**more — high pH**); pectic enzyme; sugar to OG; **acid blend to ~0.6% TA**; optional tannin | staged nutrient (more for RC212) | sulfite at bottling; optional sorbate+sulfite if back-sweetening |
| **Strawberry** | sulfite; **pectic enzyme (essential)**; sugar to OG; optional light tannin | nutrient (low-demand cool yeast) | **protect with sulfite** (oxidizes fast); sorbate+sulfite to back-sweeten; bottle promptly |
| **Blueberry** | sulfite; **pectic enzyme (essential)**; sugar to OG; optional tannin | **staged nutrient (RC212/BM4x4 high demand)** | sulfite at racking/bottling |
| **Apple (cider)** | sulfite *(skip if pasteurized + pitch now)*; optional pectic enzyme; little/no sugar; **verify juice is preservative-free** | nutrient (medium) | **sorbate ONLY if not bottle-conditioning**; back-sweeten with sorbate+sulfite for still sweet cider |
| **Blackberry** | sulfite; **pectic enzyme (essential)**; sugar to OG; **no acid blend, no added tannin** (already high) | staged nutrient (RC212/Pasteur Red) | sulfite at bottling; optional acid reducer/gelatin if austere |

## Safety & handling notes

- **Sulfite sensitivity:** a small share of people react to sulfites; keep additions to what the wine needs and note levels if sharing.
- **Sorbate + sulfite is a pair, not a choice** — using sorbate without sulfite risks geranium taint.
- **Measure, then add.** Sugar to a hydrometer, acid to a test kit, sulfite to pH. The recipes give starting amounts; the instruments give the answer.
- **Add early what must be added early.** Pectic enzyme and most tannin/sugar/acid belong pre-ferment; sorbate and finings belong at the very end.
- **Label and store dry.** Keep powders sealed, dry, and clearly labeled; sulfite and enzymes degrade with moisture and age.
