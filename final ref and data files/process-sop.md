# Process & SOP — Winemaking Reference Catalogue

**Catalogue:** Process & Standard Operating Procedure (Reference File 04)
**Scope of this edition:** The end-to-end winemaking workflow that the recipes run on — fruit prep through aging — plus measurement procedures, troubleshooting, and bottling/carbonation. The operational counterpart to the additive-focused timeline in the Additives catalogue.
**Status:** Living document — refine timings and thresholds from your own batch logs over time.

---

## How to use this file

The Fruit, Yeast, and Additives catalogues tell you *what* goes into a batch and *how much*. This file tells you *what you do, in what order, and how you measure it*. Each recipe in the Recipes catalogue follows this SOP; recipe entries only need to specify the parameters that differ (fruit, OG, yeast, temperature, additive doses).

> **Sanitation:** Every "sanitize" step below assumes a sanitized vessel, tool, and work surface. Detailed sanitizer products and procedures live in the **Sanitation reference file** — sanitize everything that touches the must or wine after the boil/heat stage.

### Glossary / conventions (process terms)

- **Must:** the unfermented fruit + water + sugar + additives mixture.
- **Primary fermentation:** the vigorous early phase, usually on the fruit, in a wide vessel.
- **Secondary fermentation:** the slower phase after the fruit is removed, in a carboy under airlock, finishing to dry.
- **Cap / punch-down:** fruit floats into a "cap" during primary; pushing it back under (punch-down) 1–2× daily extracts color/flavor and prevents spoilage.
- **Pressing / straining:** separating liquid from the fruit solids at the end of primary.
- **Racking:** siphoning clear wine off the sediment into a fresh vessel, leaving the lees behind.
- **Lees:** sediment (dead yeast, pulp). *Gross lees* = the heavy early deposit; *fine lees* = the light later deposit.
- **Degassing:** stirring/agitating to drive off dissolved CO₂ before clearing and bottling.
- **Headspace / topping up:** the air gap above the wine; minimize it (top up with similar wine or marbles) to prevent oxidation.
- **Bulk aging:** aging in carboy/keg before bottling — easier to protect and adjust than bottle aging.
- **Sugar break:** points where fermentation has consumed a fraction of the sugar (the "1/3 break" is the staged-nutrient cue).
- **SG / OG / FG / TA / pH / ABV:** as defined in the other catalogues. `ABV ≈ (OG − FG) × 131.25`.

## Master process workflow

The SOP backbone. Stage by stage, with the target/check and the catalogue each stage draws on.

| # | Stage | What you do | Target / check | Ties to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Plan & sanitize** | Choose fruit, yeast, target ABV/style. Sanitize all equipment. Prepare water (see *Water* below). | Recipe parameters set | Sanitation file; Fruit/Yeast catalogues |
| 1 | **Fruit prep** | Sort, wash, remove stems/leaves. Freeze→thaw berries to break cells. Pit cherries; keep blackberry/cherry seeds/pits intact. Crush in a straining bag. | Clean, ripe fruit; cells broken | Fruit: ratios, freeze/seed notes |
| 2 | **Must assembly** | Combine fruit, water, and sugar to target OG. Add sulfite (~50 ppm), pectic enzyme, acid blend and/or tannin per fruit. | **OG to target**; TA/pH measured | Additives: must-prep row; Fruit: OG/TA/pH |
| 3 | **Wait** | Cover loosely; hold 12–24 h so SO₂ dissipates. | — | Additives: "sulfite, then wait" |
| 4 | **Pitch** | Rehydrate yeast (with rehydration nutrient) at ~104°F, temper, pitch. Add the **first half** of the yeast nutrient. | Active fermentation within 24–48 h | Yeast: rehydration; Additives: staged nutrient |
| 5 | **Primary** | Ferment at the strain/fruit's temperature. Punch the cap down 1–2×/day. Add the **second half** of nutrient at the ~1/3 sugar break. | SG falling; ~3–7 days (1–2 wk cider) | Fruit: ferment temps; Yeast: temp ranges |
| 6 | **Press / strain** | Remove and gently press the fruit (don't crush seeds). | Solids removed | Fruit: seed/pit cautions |
| 7 | **Secondary** | Transfer to carboy under airlock; ferment to dry. | **FG ~0.995** and stable | ABV calc |
| 8 | **Rack & bulk age** | Rack off gross lees (~2–3 wk), then off fine lees (~2–3 mo). Top up headspace. Add ~25 ppm sulfite at racking. Degas. | Clearing; low headspace | Additives: racking sulfite; Fruit: aging windows |
| 9 | **Clear** | Give it time + cold settling first; add finings only if haze persists. | Bright, clear wine | Additives: finings |
| 10 | **Stabilize (if sweetening)** | On a finished, cleared wine: add potassium sorbate **+** sulfite, then back-sweeten to taste. | No active yeast; style set | Additives: sorbate; **cider exception** |
| 11 | **Bottle & carbonate** | Sanitize bottles; rack in; add bottling sulfite (~50 ppm); choose still or sparkling (prime — see below). | Filled, sealed, labeled | *Bottling* section below |
| 12 | **Age & drink** | Bottle/bulk age to the fruit's window. | Per-fruit drinking window | Fruit: aging & drinking |

> **FG "stable" rule:** before calling fermentation finished (Stage 7) or priming a still wine, confirm the same FG on 2–3 readings over 2–3 days. A still-falling gravity is not done — bottling it risks refermentation and **bottle bombs**.

> **Cider deviations:** ferment fresh juice with no water (skip most of Stage 2's water/sugar); skip must-prep sulfite if the juice is pasteurized and preservative-free and you pitch immediately; cider finishes drier than the fruit wines, so its Stage 7 target is **FG ~0.998–1.000**, not 0.995; and if bottle-conditioning for sparkle, **do not** stabilize with sorbate (Stage 10) — it blocks the carbonation. See Fruit Profiles → Apple (Hard Cider).

## Water prep

Use clean, **non-chlorinated** water. Western Washington municipal supplies are frequently treated with **chloramine** (and/or chlorine), which can cause medicinal/"band-aid" off-flavors (chlorophenols) and stress the yeast.

- **Dechlorinate with campden:** ~1 crushed campden tablet (or ~1/16 tsp K-meta) treats **~20 gallons** for dechlorination — it neutralizes both chlorine *and* chloramine. Add to the water and let stand a few minutes before use.
- **This is a different, much smaller dose than the ~50 ppm must-prep sulfite** in the Additives catalogue — don't confuse or double-count the two.
- **Alternatives:** carbon-filtered, spring, or RO water. Carbon filtering handles chlorine but is unreliable on chloramine; RO/distilled is mineral-free. Boiling drives off chlorine but **not** chloramine reliably — campden is the dependable fix for chloramine.
- Cider uses no added water, so this step is N/A for cider.

> **Sync note:** this water-prep guidance is the canonical version and is **mirrored in the Sanitation reference file**. If either is updated, update both so the dechlorination dose and method stay identical.

## Scaling

All recipes in the catalogues are written **per gallon**; scale by multiplying for your batch size.

- **Scales linearly:** fruit, sugar, water, acid blend, tannin, yeast nutrient, sulfite, pectic enzyme, sorbate, and priming sugar all multiply by the batch volume (×5 for a 5-gallon batch).
- **Does *not* scale:** OG, TA, and pH are concentrations — same targets regardless of batch size. The process steps and timings are also unchanged; only quantities and vessel sizes differ.
- **Yeast:** one 5 g dry sachet pitches up to ~5 gal. For larger batches use multiple sachets or build a starter; rehydration nutrient scales with yeast weight (~1.25 g per 1 g yeast). Liquid WLP775 needs a starter beyond ~5 gal or for high-gravity musts.
- **Vessel sizing:** size the **primary** vessel to ~1.3× batch volume to allow for the fruit cap and foam (~25–30% headspace); fill the **secondary**/aging vessel to *minimize* headspace. (Vessel specifics belong to the Equipment reference.)
- **Temperature:** larger batches retain fermentation heat and can run a few degrees warmer than the set point — monitor and cool if needed.

## Measurement procedures

The catalogues set targets in SG/OG/FG, TA, and pH — here's how to read each.

### Specific gravity & ABV (hydrometer)
Fill the test jar, float the sanitized hydrometer, spin to shed bubbles, and read at the **base of the meniscus** at eye level. Hydrometers are calibrated to a set temperature (commonly 60°F/15°C or 68°F/20°C — check yours); a warm sample reads low, so temperature-correct or cool the sample to calibration temp. Take an **OG** at must assembly and an **FG** at the end; estimate alcohol as `ABV ≈ (OG − FG) × 131.25`. For high-gravity dessert musts, the reading may exceed the scale — dilute a measured sample with an equal measured volume of water and double the points above 1.000.

### Titratable acidity (TA)
Use an acid test kit. In brief: measure a small filtered sample, add distilled water and the indicator (phenolphthalein), and titrate with the kit's standardized base (NaOH) until the color change holds; convert per the kit's instructions to **% tartaric**. Target most table wines to ~0.55–0.70%. Adjust pre-ferment toward target with acid blend (low) or acid reducer (high); fine-tune to taste before bottling. Red/dark musts make the endpoint hard to see — a pH meter to an 8.2 endpoint is more reliable for those.

### pH
Calibrate the meter with pH 4.0 and 7.0 buffers, rinse with distilled water, and read at room temperature. pH drives **sulfite dosing** — see the sulfite-by-pH table in the Additives catalogue. High-pH musts (notably sweet cherry) need more free SO₂ and benefit from an acid addition to pull pH into a safer range.

## Troubleshooting

*Quick in-process reference. The full faults catalogue — with symptoms, causes, fixes, and a diagnostic index — is the **Troubleshooting & Faults** file.*

| Problem | Likely cause | What to do | Ties to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stuck / sluggish ferment** (gravity stalls high) | Too cold; nutrient-starved; ABV beyond yeast tolerance; underpitched | Warm gently into range; add yeast energizer; confirm target ≤ strain tolerance; if needed, build an EC-1118 starter and acclimate the stuck wine into it gradually | Yeast: tolerances, EC-1118; Additives: energizer |
| **Rotten-egg / sulfur (H₂S)** during ferment | Nitrogen deficiency / yeast stress | Add nutrient/energizer early; gentle aeration in the first third only; if it lingers post-ferment, splash-rack to aerate | Yeast: RC212/BM4x4 demand; Additives: nutrient |
| **Browning / sherry / flat oxidized notes** | Oxygen exposure; low SO₂; headspace | Minimize headspace and topping up; maintain free SO₂; avoid splashing after fermentation | Additives: sulfite at racking/bottling |
| **Persistent haze** | Pectin (enzyme added too late) or protein/yeast | Pectin haze is hard to fix post-ferment — prevent by adding pectic enzyme pre-ferment; otherwise give time + cold, then fine | Additives: pectic enzyme, finings |
| **Vinegar / nail-polish (acetic, VA)** | *Acetobacter* + oxygen, often via fruit flies | Largely preventable, not curable once strong: keep SO₂ up, headspace low, vessels sealed, fruit flies out | Additives: sulfite |
| **Geranium off-smell** | Sorbate used **without** co-dosed sulfite + stray bacteria | Preventable only — always pair sorbate with sulfite | Additives: sorbate (two-rule note) |
| **Too sweet / won't finish dry** | Stuck (see above) or yeast at tolerance | Address stuck causes, or accept the style and stabilize | Yeast: tolerances |
| **Too sharp / austere** (esp. blackberry) | High TA | Acid reducer in small steps; cold-stabilize; time; slight back-sweetening | Additives: acid reducer; Fruit: blackberry |

## Bottling & carbonation

### Closures & bottles
Still wines: standard wine bottles with corks (or screw caps/Vino-Seal). Sparkling cider: **only pressure-rated bottles** — Champagne bottles, swing-top (Grolsch) bottles, or beer bottles with crown caps. Never bottle a sparkling product in standard wine bottles.

### Still
No priming. Confirm the wine is fully dry-and-stable (or stabilized + back-sweetened per Stage 10), add bottling sulfite (~50 ppm), bottle, and age.

### Sparkling (cider, bottle-conditioned)
Start from a **fully fermented, dry** cider (so you control the exact carbonation). Prime with roughly **1 oz (~28 g) dextrose per gallon** for a moderate sparkle (~2.2–2.5 volumes of CO₂), mixed evenly into the whole batch before filling. Cap in pressure-rated bottles, condition ~2 weeks at room temperature, then refrigerate.

> **Bottle-bomb safety — the non-negotiables:** (1) never prime a wine whose FG is still falling; (2) never combine priming with potassium sorbate; (3) use only pressure-rated bottles for anything carbonated; (4) if uncertain whether fermentation is truly finished, do not bottle a still wine with residual fermentable sugar. Refrigerate to slow any surprise activity.
