# Equipment — Winemaking Reference Catalogue

**Catalogue:** Equipment (Reference File 05)
**Scope of this edition:** The physical gear the process runs on — fermentation vessels and sizing, fruit-prep tools, transfer/racking gear, measurement instruments, temperature control, and bottling/carbonation hardware. The hardware counterpart to the Process & SOP catalogue.
**Status:** Living document — refine from your own setup and batch logs over time.

---

## How to use this file

The Process & SOP catalogue tells you *what you do*; this file tells you *what you do it with*, and how to size it to your batch. Each item lists what it is, what to get and how to size it, and which process step or catalogue it serves. Sanitizer **products and procedures** are not here — they live in the Sanitation reference file; this file lists the cleaning *gear* only.

### Glossary / conventions

- **Primary fermenter:** the wide vessel (usually a food-grade bucket) used during vigorous primary fermentation, often with the fruit; needs headroom for the cap and foam.
- **Secondary / aging vessel:** the narrow-necked vessel (carboy, demijohn, jug) the wine finishes and ages in under airlock; filled to minimize headspace.
- **Carboy:** a large narrow-necked bottle (glass or PET), typically 3–6 gal.
- **Demijohn / jug:** smaller narrow-necked vessel (1–3 gal); a 1-gal glass jug is the standard small-batch secondary.
- **Airlock:** a one-way valve (3-piece or S-shaped "bubbler") that lets CO₂ out while keeping air/contaminants out.
- **Bung / stopper:** the rubber plug (drilled for the airlock) that fits the vessel neck; buckets use a grommeted lid instead.
- **Headspace:** the air gap above the wine — wanted in primary (cap/foam room), minimized everywhere after.
- **Muselet:** the wire cage that secures a champagne cork against pressure.

### The SOP sizing rule (carried over)

Size the **primary** vessel to **~1.3× batch volume** (room for the fruit cap and foam). Fill the **secondary/aging** vessel to *minimize* headspace. See Process & SOP → Scaling.

## Equipment by category

### Fermentation vessels

- **Primary fermenter:** a food-grade (HDPE) bucket with a grommeted lid, ideally **wide-mouth** so you can add fruit and punch the cap down. Size to ~1.3× batch (Stage 5 generates a floating fruit cap and foam). A lidded bucket also makes daily cap punch-downs easy.
- **Secondary / aging vessel:** a narrow-necked **glass carboy or 1-gal jug** (or PET "Better Bottle"). The narrow neck takes a drilled bung + airlock and minimizes the wine's air contact.
- **Glass vs. PET:** glass is inert, scratch-resistant, and best for **long bulk aging** (your blueberry/blackberry/cherry reds that age 1–2 years); it's heavy and breakable. PET is lighter and won't shatter but scratches easily (scratches harbor microbes) and is slightly oxygen-permeable over long aging — fine for shorter holds (strawberry, cider).
- **Ties to:** SOP Stages 5/7/8; Fruit Profiles aging windows (long agers → glass).
- **Watch-outs:** food-grade only; avoid scratched or odor-retaining plastic; you need *two* narrow-neck vessels minimum so you have somewhere to rack *to*.

### Airlocks, bungs & lids

- An **airlock** (3-piece or S-shaped) plus a **drilled rubber bung** sized to the carboy neck, or a **grommeted bucket lid** for the primary. Fill the airlock to its line with sanitizer solution, cooled boiled water, or cheap vodka.
- **Ties to:** every stage under airlock (Stages 7–10).
- **Watch-outs:** keep the airlock topped up (it can dry out or get sucked back on temperature swings); match bung size to the specific vessel neck.

### Fruit-prep tools

- **Kitchen/bench scale** (reads lb and kg): essential — the recipes are by **weight per gallon** (fruit ratios, sugar). Don't eyeball.
- **Masher** (sturdy potato masher) or sanitized hands for berries/cherries; for **apples** you need a **crusher/scratter** to pulp, then a **press**.
- **Straining / brewing bag** (fine nylon mesh): holds the fruit in the primary and makes pressing/lifting clean — the "straining bag" every fruit recipe references.
- **Fruit press** (basket/ratchet press): worthwhile mainly for **cider** (pressing juice from apples). For berries and cherries, a straining bag + hand-squeeze is enough at home scale.
- **Ties to:** Fruit Profiles ratios, freeze/thaw and seed/pit cautions, "straining bag"; cider's fresh-pressed-juice requirement.
- **Watch-outs:** don't crush blackberry seeds or cherry pits during mashing/pressing (bitterness/benzaldehyde) — press gently.

### Transfer & racking

- **Auto-siphon or racking cane** + **food-grade tubing** (~5/16"–3/8"): for racking clear wine off the lees without disturbing sediment (Stage 8).
- **Bottling wand** (spring-tip): attaches to the tubing and fills bottles to the right level with minimal splashing/oxygen pickup.
- **Wide funnel** with a removable strainer: for transfers and catching solids.
- **Ties to:** SOP Stage 8 racking; Stage 11 bottling.
- **Watch-outs:** keep the cane tip above the sediment; avoid splashing after fermentation (oxidation).

### Measurement instruments

These are exactly what the SOP **Measurement procedures** require:

- **Hydrometer** (triple-scale: SG / Brix / potential alcohol) **+ a tall test jar**: for OG and FG, and your ABV via `(OG − FG) × 131.25`. Note its calibration temperature.
- **Refractometer** (optional): handy for quick **Brix of fresh fruit/juice before fermentation**, but it reads inaccurately once alcohol is present — use the **hydrometer** for FG.
- **Acid test kit** (titration — base + phenolphthalein + syringe): for TA as % tartaric.
- **pH meter + calibration buffers (4.0 and 7.0) + storage solution** (pH strips are a low-precision fallback): pH drives sulfite dosing.
- **Thermometer** (digital probe and/or a stick-on LCD strip on the vessel): for the ~104°F yeast rehydration and for tracking ferment temperature.
- **Ties to:** SOP Measurement procedures; Additives sulfite-by-pH table; Yeast rehydration (~104°F).
- **Watch-outs:** calibrate the pH meter before each session; temperature-correct hydrometer readings; refractometers need correction post-ferment (so don't trust them for FG).

### Temperature control

PNW garages/basements swing seasonally, and the catalogues set strain- and fruit-specific temperatures — so some control matters.

- **Heating (cool months):** a **brew belt** or **heat pad/mat**, or simply a warmer interior spot — to keep warm-loving reds (RC212, Pasteur Red, BM4x4) in range.
- **Cooling (warm months / cool ferments):** a **swamp cooler** (vessel standing in a water bath with a wet towel), a cool basement, or a **fridge/freezer with an external temperature controller** (e.g. Inkbird-type) — needed to hold the **cool, aroma-preserving ferments** (strawberry on QA23/Côte des Blancs at 60–68°F).
- **Ties to:** Fruit Profiles ferment temps; Yeast Strains temperature ranges; SOP Scaling (larger batches self-heat).
- **Watch-outs:** measure the *must* temperature, not room temperature — active fermentation runs warmer than ambient, more so in larger batches.

### Bottling & closures

- **Still wines:** standard **750 mL wine bottles** + **corks** (#8/#9) + a **corker** (hand or floor; a floor corker is far easier), or screw-cap/Vino-Seal closures.
- **Sparkling cider (bottle-conditioned):** **pressure-rated bottles only** — **Champagne bottles** with champagne corks + **muselet/wire cages**, **swing-top (Grolsch) bottles**, or **beer bottles** with **crown caps + a capper** (bench or wing).
- **Bottle brush** and a **bottle tree/rinser** for cleaning and draining.
- **Ties to:** SOP Stage 11 and the bottling-safety block — never bottle anything carbonated in standard wine bottles.
- **Watch-outs:** match cork size to bottle bore; for sparkling, the muselet/cap and a pressure-rated bottle are non-negotiable (bottle-bomb risk).

### Cleaning gear (products → Sanitation file)

- **Carboy brush** and **bottle brush**, a **spray bottle** for sanitizer, and a **vessel for sanitizer soak**.
- **Ties to:** Sanitation reference file for the actual cleaners/sanitizers and procedures — this file lists only the physical brushes/containers.
- **Watch-outs:** brushes can scratch PET (favor non-abrasive); keep cleaning gear separate from kitchen tools.

### Optional / nice-to-have

- **Degassing tool** (drill-mounted stirrer or vacuum system): speeds the Stage 8 degassing.
- **Spare narrow-neck vessel:** you always need a clean vessel to rack *into*.
- **Labels + marker + batch-log notebook:** to run the SOP **batch log template**.

## Equipment by batch size

Sizing for the two common home batches (the recipes are per-gallon; see SOP → Scaling).

| Item | 1-gallon batch | 5-gallon batch |
|---|---|---|
| Primary fermenter | ~2 gal bucket | ~6.5–7.5 gal bucket |
| Secondary / aging | 1 gal glass jug | 5–6 gal carboy (top up headspace) |
| Airlock + bung/lid | 1 set | 1 set (+ spare) |
| Straining bag | 1 (small) | 1 (large) |
| Dry yeast | 1 × 5 g sachet | 1 × 5 g sachet (covers ~5 gal) |
| Spare vessel to rack into | 1 (jug) | 1 (carboy) |
| 750 mL bottles | ~5 | ~25 |
| Hydrometer, test jar, acid kit, pH meter, thermometer, auto-siphon, tubing, bottling wand | same kit | same kit |

> Yield note: after racking off lees you lose some volume, so a 5-gal must often bottles ~4.5–4.75 gal (~23–24 bottles) and the secondary will need topping up. Plan fruit and bottle counts with a little margin.
