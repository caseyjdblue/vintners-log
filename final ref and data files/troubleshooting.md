# Troubleshooting & Faults — Winemaking Reference Catalogue

**Catalogue:** Troubleshooting & Faults (Reference File 09)
**Scope of this edition:** The comprehensive faults catalogue — fermentation, aroma/flavor, clarity, microbial, body/balance, and packaging problems — each with symptoms, causes, fix, and prevention. Expanded from the quick table in the Process & SOP catalogue.
**Status:** Living document — refine from your own batch logs over time.

## How to use this file

This is the **canonical, full** faults reference; the Process & SOP catalogue keeps a short subset for quick in-process use. Start with the **diagnostic index** to jump from a symptom to the likely fault, then read its entry. Two themes run through almost every fix: **sanitation** (most faults are preventable spoilage — Sanitation catalogue) and **measurement** (diagnose with hydrometer/pH/taste, not guesswork). Many faults are far easier to *prevent* than to *cure* — prevention is called out in every entry.

## A. Fermentation problems

### A1 — Slow or no start
- **Symptoms:** no airlock activity 24–48 h after pitching.
- **Causes:** dead/old yeast, cold must, under-pitched, residual sulfite (pitched too soon after must-prep Campden).
- **Fix:** confirm temperature is in the strain's range (warm gently if cold); repitch properly **rehydrated** yeast (with rehydration nutrient); ensure 12–24 h passed since must-prep sulfite.
- **Prevention:** rehydrate at ~104°F, pitch into range, wait the sulfite-out window. (Yeast Strains → rehydration.)

### A2 — Stuck or sluggish ferment
- **Symptoms:** gravity stalls above expected FG and stops moving.
- **Causes:** too cold; **nutrient starvation**; target ABV beyond yeast tolerance; osmotic stress (very high OG).
- **Fix:** warm into range; add **yeast energizer**; verify target ≤ strain tolerance; for a true restart, build an **EC-1118** starter and acclimate the stuck wine into it gradually.
- **Prevention:** stage the nutrient (½ at pitch, ½ at 1/3 break — critical for RC212/BM4x4); set OG within tolerance; step-feed high-ABV musts. (Yeast Strains; Additives → nutrient.)

### A3 — Violent ferment / foaming over
- **Symptoms:** cap blows out, airlock clogs, mess.
- **Causes:** too warm; primary vessel too full.
- **Fix:** cool toward the low end of range; use a blow-off tube; reduce fill.
- **Prevention:** size primary to ~1.3× batch; control temperature. (Equipment; Calculations → vessel sizing.)

## B. Aroma & flavor faults

### B1 — H₂S / rotten-egg sulfur
- **Symptoms:** rotten-egg smell during/after ferment.
- **Causes:** nitrogen-deficient, stressed yeast (classic with under-fed RC212/BM4x4).
- **Fix:** add nutrient/energizer; **gentle aeration in the first third only**; if it lingers post-ferment, splash-rack to aerate.
- **Prevention:** stage nutrient; pick adequate-nutrient strains. (Additives; Yeast Strains.)

### B2 — Oxidation
- **Symptoms:** sherry/nutty notes, browning, flat aromatics.
- **Causes:** oxygen exposure, low free SO₂, excess headspace, splashing after fermentation.
- **Fix:** largely preventive; minimize further exposure, top up, maintain free SO₂.
- **Prevention:** top up to minimize headspace, hold free SO₂ for pH, don't splash post-ferment. Strawberry is especially prone (drink young). (Calculations → free SO₂ by pH; Fruit → strawberry.)

### B3 — Acetic / ethyl acetate (volatile acidity)
- **Symptoms:** vinegar or nail-polish-remover smell.
- **Causes:** *Acetobacter* + oxygen, often introduced by fruit flies.
- **Fix:** **not curable** once pronounced — discard or blend away trace amounts.
- **Prevention:** sanitation, low headspace, sealed vessels, exclude fruit flies, keep SO₂ up. (Sanitation.)

### B4 — Geranium off-smell
- **Symptoms:** crushed-geranium-leaf odor.
- **Causes:** potassium **sorbate used without co-dosed sulfite** + lactic bacteria.
- **Fix:** none — preventable only.
- **Prevention:** always pair sorbate with sulfite when stabilizing. (Additives → sorbate; Sanitation.)

### B5 — Medicinal / "band-aid" (chlorophenol)
- **Symptoms:** plastic, medicinal, band-aid character.
- **Causes:** chlorine/**chloramine** in water reacting with phenols; bleach sanitizer residue.
- **Fix:** preventable only.
- **Prevention:** dechlorinate water (~1 campden/20 gal) or use RO/spring water; avoid bleach. (SOP/Sanitation → Water prep.)

### B6 — Fusel / hot / harsh
- **Symptoms:** solventy, hot, headache-y alcohol character.
- **Causes:** fermentation too warm for the strain.
- **Fix:** time and aging soften it somewhat; not fully reversible.
- **Prevention:** ferment in the strain's range; cool larger batches (they self-heat). (Yeast Strains; Equipment → temperature control.)

> **Over-sulfited** (sharp SO₂ nose / matchstick): dose to the pH table, not by habit; if over-dosed, time and gentle aeration reduce it. (Calculations → sulfite.)

## C. Clarity problems

### C1 — Pectin haze
- **Symptoms:** persistent milky haze that won't drop.
- **Causes:** pectin not broken down (enzyme skipped or added too late).
- **Fix:** hard to clear post-ferment (alcohol inhibits the enzyme); a finings regimen may help partially.
- **Prevention:** add **pectic enzyme pre-ferment** — essential for these high-pectin fruits. (Additives → pectic enzyme.)

### C2 — Protein / yeast haze
- **Symptoms:** cloudiness that slowly settles.
- **Fix:** give it time + cold settling first; then fine (bentonite / kieselsol+chitosan / Sparkolloid); rack off the lees.
- **Prevention:** healthy ferment, patience, cold conditioning. (Additives → finings.)

### C3 — Won't clear at all
- **Causes:** combination of the above, or ongoing slight fermentation.
- **Fix:** confirm FG is stable (not still fermenting), cold-condition, then fine; check for pectin specifically.
- **Prevention:** finish dry-and-stable, cold settle, fine as needed.

## D. Microbial spoilage / surface growth

### D1 — Film yeast (flor / *Mycoderma*)
- **Symptoms:** thin white/grey film on the surface.
- **Causes:** oxygen + low alcohol + low SO₂ (common in low-ABV cider).
- **Fix:** rack from beneath the film, top up, add sulfite; discard if extensive.
- **Prevention:** low headspace, adequate SO₂, full vessels. (Sanitation.)

### D2 — Mold
- **Symptoms:** fuzzy growth on must surface or in gear.
- **Causes:** spoiled fruit, poor sanitation, scratched plastic.
- **Fix:** discard affected must; do not salvage moldy fruit.
- **Prevention:** clean ripe fruit, sanitation, replace scratched plastic. (Sanitation; Fruit prep.)

### D3 — Ropiness (lactic)
- **Symptoms:** slimy, oily, pours like a thread.
- **Causes:** lactic bacteria.
- **Fix:** sometimes recoverable with SO₂ + stirring/agitation; often best discarded.
- **Prevention:** sanitation + sulfite. (Sanitation.)

## E. Color, body & balance

### E1 — Color loss / fading
- **Symptoms:** red/pink fading to orange-brown (notably strawberry).
- **Causes:** oxidation and naturally unstable pigments.
- **Fix:** preventive; blend with a more stable red fruit if needed.
- **Prevention:** minimize oxygen, drink young, consider a rhubarb/red-fruit blend. (Fruit → strawberry; Recipes 3–4.)

### E2 — Too sweet / won't finish dry
- **Causes:** stuck ferment (see A2) or yeast at tolerance.
- **Fix:** resolve the stuck causes, or accept the style and **stabilize** (sorbate + sulfite).
- **Prevention:** match OG to yeast tolerance. (Yeast Strains; Calculations.)

### E3 — Too sharp / austere or too tannic
- **Symptoms:** aggressive acidity or drying astringency (often young blackberry).
- **Fix:** time/aging softens both; **acid reducer** in small steps for acidity; slight back-sweetening (stabilize first); gelatin fining trims excess tannin.
- **Prevention:** measure TA before adding acid; don't over-tannin; age the structured reds. (Additives → acid reducer, finings; Fruit → blackberry.)

## F. Packaging problems

### F1 — Refermentation in bottle / bottle bombs
- **Symptoms:** unexpected fizz, gushing, or exploding bottles.
- **Causes:** bottled with residual fermentable sugar while not stabilized; primed a wine still fermenting; sorbate omitted before back-sweetening.
- **Fix:** **refrigerate immediately** to slow activity; handle suspect bottles with care.
- **Prevention:** confirm **stable FG** before bottling; stabilize (sorbate + sulfite) before back-sweetening; for sparkling, prime a fully-dry base and use **pressure-rated bottles only**. (SOP → Bottling; Calculations → priming.)

### F2 — Leaking or pushed-out cork
- **Causes:** refermentation pressure, oversized/under-driven cork, dry cork.
- **Fix:** address any refermentation (F1); re-cork sound bottles.
- **Prevention:** stable wine, correct cork size, a proper corker. (Equipment → bottling.)

### F3 — Under-carbonated sparkling cider
- **Causes:** too little priming sugar, cool conditioning, weak/insufficient yeast at bottling.
- **Fix:** condition longer at room temperature; ensure viable yeast remained at bottling.
- **Prevention:** prime at ~1 oz dextrose/gal, condition ~2 weeks warm before refrigerating. (Calculations → priming; Recipes 8.)
