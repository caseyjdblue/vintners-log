# Recipes — Winemaking Reference Catalogue

**Catalogue:** Recipes (Reference File 07)
**Scope of this edition:** Full per-batch recipes for all five fruits — 11 recipes across table, dessert/port, off-dry, blend, and cider/apple-wine styles. The convergence file: every recipe draws on the Fruit, Yeast, Additives, Process & SOP, Equipment, and Sanitation catalogues.
**Status:** Living document — refine amounts and timings from your own batch logs over time.

---

## How to use this file

Each recipe runs **on the Process & SOP workflow** and assumes the **Sanitation** routine — it does not re-teach either. A recipe lists only what's batch-specific: the spec, the ingredients, the additive schedule by stage, and the callouts that differ from the default process. To make any recipe:

1. **Sanitize everything** that will touch the must or wine (Sanitation catalogue).
2. **Follow the SOP stages 0–12**; the recipe's "Method" highlights only the steps that differ.
3. **Measure, don't guess:** confirm OG/TA/pH (SOP → Measurement procedures), and confirm **FG is stable over 2–3 days** before bottling.
4. **Scale** from the per-gallon amounts per SOP → Scaling.

### Conventions

- All recipes are **per 1 US gallon** unless noted; multiply for your batch (×5 for 5 gal).
- **Additive schedule** follows the Additives master timeline; doses are per gallon.
- `ABV ≈ (OG − FG) × 131.25`. Sugar rule: ~1 lb cane sugar in 1 gal ≈ +0.045 SG — add gradually to the target OG with a hydrometer.
- **Three sulfite uses** (Sanitation): the ~50 ppm here is must/aging/bottling protection, *not* the dechlorination or sanitizing dose.
- "Stabilize" = potassium sorbate **+** sulfite on a finished, cleared wine before back-sweetening (never during fermentation; never on a cider you intend to bottle-condition).

## Sweet Cherry

### 1 — Sweet Cherry Table Wine
*Deep, off-dry-to-dry table red. Cherry is low-acid, so this recipe adds acid for balance.*

**Spec (per gal):** OG ~1.090 · FG ~0.995 · ABV ~12% · fruit 3.5 lb · yeast 71B · ferment 65–72°F · target TA ~0.60%, pH watch (high) · drink 6–12 mo+

**Ingredients:** 3.5 lb sweet cherries (pitted; a few whole pits optional for subtle almond — never crush them) · ~1.75–2 lb cane sugar (to OG ~1.090) · water to 1 gal · 1 crushed Campden tablet · 1/2 tsp pectic enzyme · ~1–1.5 tsp acid blend (to ~0.60% TA — to measurement) · 1 tsp yeast nutrient · optional 1/4 tsp wine tannin · 71B yeast (+ rehydration nutrient)

**Additive schedule:** Must prep — Campden ~50 ppm, pectic enzyme, sugar to OG, acid blend, optional tannin → wait 12–24 h. Pitch — ½ nutrient. 1/3 break — remaining ½ nutrient. Racking — ~25 ppm sulfite. Bottling — ~50 ppm sulfite.

**Method (deltas from SOP):** Pit cherries; primary on the fruit 5–7 days, punch cap daily. Because pH is high, be strict on sanitation and sulfite (Additives sulfite-by-pH). Press, secondary to dry, bulk age. Long ager — give it a year for the tannin/color to integrate.

### 2 — Sweet Cherry Dessert (Port-Style)
*Rich, sweet, high-ABV. Step-fed to reach strength, finished sweet.*

**Spec (per gal):** OG start ~1.100 (step-fed higher) · FG varies (finished sweet) · ABV ~17–18% · fruit 4 lb · yeast EC-1118 · ferment 68–75°F · drink 1 yr+

**Ingredients:** 4 lb sweet cherries (pitted) · cane sugar in stages (see method) · water to 1 gal · 1 crushed Campden tablet · 1/2 tsp pectic enzyme · ~1 tsp acid blend · 1 tsp yeast nutrient · 1/2 tsp yeast energizer · EC-1118 yeast (+ rehydration nutrient)

**Additive schedule:** Must prep — Campden ~50 ppm, pectic enzyme, acid blend, **initial** sugar to OG ~1.100 → wait 12–24 h. Pitch — ½ nutrient + energizer. 1/3 break — remaining ½ nutrient. **Step-feed:** as gravity drops toward ~1.010–1.020, add ~3–4 oz sugar/gal, dissolved, in 2–3 additions, until the ferment slows near EC-1118's ~18% tolerance and leaves residual sweetness. Stabilize — sorbate + sulfite; back-sweeten to taste if needed. Bottling — ~50 ppm sulfite.

**Method (deltas):** Step-feeding avoids osmotic shock from a single huge sugar charge. Expect a slow, long ferment. This is a *fermented* dessert wine (no spirit fortification). Age at least a year.

## Strawberry

### 3 — Light Strawberry Wine
*Delicate, fresh, off-dry — built to drink young before color and aroma fade.*

**Spec (per gal):** OG ~1.082 · FG ~0.995 · ABV ~10–11% · fruit 3.5–4 lb · yeast QA23 (or Côte des Blancs) · ferment cool 60–68°F · drink 3–6 mo

**Ingredients:** 3.5–4 lb ripe strawberries (hulled, mashed in a straining bag) · ~1.75 lb cane sugar (to OG ~1.082) · water to 1 gal · 1 crushed Campden tablet · 1/2 tsp pectic enzyme (essential) · 1 tsp yeast nutrient · optional 1/4 tsp wine tannin · QA23 yeast (+ rehydration nutrient)

**Additive schedule:** Must prep — Campden ~50 ppm, pectic enzyme, sugar to OG, optional tannin → wait 12–24 h. Pitch — ½ nutrient. 1/3 break — remaining ½ nutrient. Racking — ~25 ppm sulfite, **top up to minimize headspace**. Bottling — ~50 ppm sulfite, bottle promptly.

**Method (deltas):** **Ferment cool** to preserve aroma. Primary only 3–5 days on the fruit. Protect aggressively from oxygen — strawberry browns and fades fast. Don't long-age.

### 4 — Strawberry–Rhubarb Wine
*Rhubarb adds acid, body, and color stability to delicate strawberry.*

**Spec (per gal):** OG ~1.085 · FG ~0.995 · ABV ~10–11% · fruit 3 lb strawberry + 1 lb rhubarb (stalks) · yeast QA23 · ferment cool 60–68°F · drink 3–9 mo

**Ingredients:** 3 lb strawberries + 1 lb rhubarb **stalks only** (chopped) · ~1.75 lb cane sugar (to OG ~1.085) · water to 1 gal · 1 crushed Campden tablet · 1/2 tsp pectic enzyme · 1 tsp yeast nutrient · QA23 yeast (+ rehydration nutrient)

**Additive schedule:** As recipe 3. Rhubarb supplies acid, so measure TA before adding any acid blend (usually none needed).

**Method (deltas):** **Rhubarb leaves are toxic (oxalic acid) — use stalks only, never the leaves.** Freeze/chop rhubarb to soften. Ferment cool; the blend holds color better than straight strawberry but still drinks best within the year.

## Blueberry

### 5 — Blueberry Dry Red-Style
*The flagship — deep, structured, ages like a light red.*

**Spec (per gal):** OG ~1.090 · FG ~0.995 · ABV ~12% · fruit 2.5–3 lb · yeast RC212 (high nutrient demand) · ferment 68–74°F · drink 6–12 mo+ (to 2 yr)

**Ingredients:** 2.5–3 lb blueberries (frozen then thawed, lightly crushed in a bag) · ~2 lb cane sugar (to OG ~1.090) · water to 1 gal · 1 crushed Campden tablet · 1/2 tsp pectic enzyme (essential) · 1 tsp yeast nutrient · optional 1/4 tsp wine tannin · RC212 yeast (+ rehydration nutrient)

**Additive schedule:** Must prep — Campden ~50 ppm, pectic enzyme, sugar to OG, optional tannin → wait 12–24 h. Pitch — ½ nutrient. **1/3 break — remaining ½ nutrient (critical for RC212 to avoid H₂S/stuck).** Racking — ~25 ppm sulfite. Bottling — ~50 ppm sulfite.

**Method (deltas):** **Freeze/thaw the fruit first** — biggest quality lever. Primary 5–7 days, punch cap daily for color. RC212 wants warmth and feeding — stage the nutrient. Bulk age; rewards patience.

### 6 — Blueberry Off-Dry
*Softer, fruit-forward, back-sweetened — demonstrates the stabilize-then-sweeten flow.*

**Spec (per gal):** OG ~1.088 · ferment dry then back-sweetened to ~1.006 · ABV ~11–12% · fruit 2.5–3 lb · yeast 71B · ferment 64–74°F · drink within ~1 yr

**Ingredients:** as recipe 5 but **71B yeast** · plus for finishing: potassium sorbate ~1/2 tsp + 1 crushed Campden tablet, and cane sugar (or blueberry juice) to back-sweeten

**Additive schedule:** Through fermentation as recipe 5 (71B is low–medium demand; still stage the nutrient). **Stabilize** — once dry, cleared, and racked: sorbate + sulfite, then **back-sweeten** to ~1.006 SG to taste. Bottling — ~50 ppm sulfite.

**Method (deltas):** Ferment fully dry and confirm FG stable, **then** stabilize before adding any sugar — sweetening an unstable wine risks refermentation/bottle bombs. 71B keeps it fruit-forward; drink younger than the dry version.

## Apple (Cider & Apple Wine)

### 7 — Traditional Dry Hard Cider
*Fresh-pressed juice, no added water, crisp and dry. Still version.*

**Spec (per gal):** OG ~1.050 · FG ~0.998–1.000 · ABV ~6–7% · fresh juice only · yeast M02 (fruity) or EC-1118 (dry/crisp) · ferment 60–68°F (M02/EC-1118) or **68–72°F if using WLP775** · drink 1–3 mo+

**Ingredients:** 1 gal fresh-pressed, **preservative-free** apple juice (blend sweet/sharp/bittersweet varieties), OG ~1.050 · 1 crushed Campden tablet *(skip if juice is pasteurized and you pitch immediately)* · 1 tsp yeast nutrient · optional 1/4 tsp pectic enzyme for clarity · cider yeast or EC-1118 (+ rehydration nutrient)

**Additive schedule:** Must prep — Campden ~50 ppm (or skip per above), no water/sugar → wait 24 h. Pitch — ½ nutrient. 1/3 break — remaining ½ nutrient. Racking — ~25 ppm sulfite. Bottling — ~50 ppm sulfite.

**Method (deltas):** **Confirm juice has no potassium sorbate/benzoate** or it won't ferment. Primary 1–2 weeks; ferment cool/clean. Cider finishes drier (FG ~0.998–1.000). Leave still.

### 8 — Sparkling Bottle-Conditioned Cider
*The dry cider above, carbonated naturally in the bottle.*

**Spec (per gal):** as recipe 7, **plus priming** · drink 1–3 mo+

**Ingredients:** as recipe 7 · plus **~1 oz (~28 g) dextrose per gallon** for priming · **pressure-rated bottles** (champagne/swing-top/beer + caps)

**Additive schedule:** as recipe 7 through bottling — **but no sorbate/stabilization** (it would block carbonation).

**Method (deltas):** Start from a **fully fermented, dry** cider (confirm stable FG). Mix the priming dextrose evenly into the whole batch, bottle in **pressure-rated bottles only**, condition ~2 weeks at room temp, then refrigerate. **Bottle-bomb safety:** never prime a wine still dropping in gravity; never combine priming with sorbate; pressure-rated bottles only (SOP → Bottling & carbonation).

### 9 — Apple Wine
*Chaptalized to table-wine strength — a different, more wine-like product than cider.*

**Spec (per gal):** OG ~1.085–1.090 · FG ~0.995 · ABV ~11–12% · fresh juice + sugar · yeast 71B (soft) or EC-1118 (dry/crisp) · ferment 64–72°F · drink 6–12 mo

**Ingredients:** 1 gal fresh-pressed apple juice · cane sugar to raise OG to ~1.085–1.090 · 1 crushed Campden tablet *(skip per pasteurized rule)* · 1 tsp yeast nutrient · optional 1/4 tsp pectic enzyme · 71B or EC-1118 (+ rehydration nutrient)

**Additive schedule:** Must prep — Campden ~50 ppm (or skip), **sugar to OG ~1.085–1.090** → wait 24 h. Pitch — ½ nutrient. 1/3 break — remaining ½ nutrient. Racking — ~25 ppm sulfite. Bottling — ~50 ppm sulfite. Optional: stabilize + back-sweeten for an off-dry style.

**Method (deltas):** Unlike cider, this adds sugar for higher ABV and ages longer like a wine. Confirm FG stable; bulk age a few months minimum.

## Blackberry

### 10 — Blackberry Dry Red-Style
*Robust, deeply colored, tannic — excellent foraged-fruit red.*

**Spec (per gal):** OG ~1.090 · FG ~0.995 · ABV ~12–13% · fruit 3.5–4 lb · yeast RC212 or Pasteur Red · ferment 68–74°F · drink 6–12 mo+ (to 2 yr)

**Ingredients:** 3.5–4 lb blackberries (frozen then thawed, crushed in a bag — **do not crush the seeds**) · ~2 lb cane sugar (to OG ~1.090) · water to 1 gal · 1 crushed Campden tablet · 1/2 tsp pectic enzyme (essential) · 1 tsp yeast nutrient · RC212 yeast (+ rehydration nutrient)

**Additive schedule:** Must prep — Campden ~50 ppm, pectic enzyme, sugar to OG → wait 12–24 h. **No acid blend and no added tannin** (blackberry is already high in both — measure first). Pitch — ½ nutrient. **1/3 break — remaining ½ nutrient (RC212).** Racking — ~25 ppm sulfite. Bottling — ~50 ppm sulfite.

**Method (deltas):** Freeze/thaw; primary 5–7 days, punch cap daily. **Don't crush the seeds** (bitterness); strain thoroughly afterward. If the young wine tastes austere, consider a small acid-reducer addition or slight back-sweetening (stabilize first).

### 11 — Blackberry Dessert (Port-Style)
*Sweet, high-ABV, age-worthy — blackberry's natural port affinity.*

**Spec (per gal):** OG start ~1.100 (step-fed) · FG varies (finished sweet) · ABV ~17% · fruit 4 lb · yeast EC-1118 · ferment 68–75°F · drink 1 yr+

**Ingredients:** 4 lb blackberries (frozen/thawed) · cane sugar in stages · water to 1 gal · 1 crushed Campden tablet · 1/2 tsp pectic enzyme · 1 tsp yeast nutrient · 1/2 tsp yeast energizer · EC-1118 yeast (+ rehydration nutrient)

**Additive schedule:** Must prep — Campden ~50 ppm, pectic enzyme, **initial** sugar to OG ~1.100 → wait 12–24 h. Pitch — ½ nutrient + energizer. 1/3 break — remaining ½ nutrient. **Step-feed** sugar (~3–4 oz/gal in 2–3 additions as gravity drops toward ~1.010–1.020) toward EC-1118's ~18% tolerance. Stabilize — sorbate + sulfite; back-sweeten to taste. Bottling — ~50 ppm sulfite.

**Method (deltas):** As the cherry dessert (recipe 2): step-feed to avoid osmotic stress; a *fermented* dessert wine, not fortified. Blackberry's tannin gives it real structure — age a year+.
